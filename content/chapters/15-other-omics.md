<h2>Epitranscriptomics</h2>
<p>More than 170 RNA modifications are known. <strong>m6A</strong> (N6-methyladenosine), written by METTL3/METTL14, read by YTHDF/YTHDC proteins and erased by FTO/ALKBH5, affects mRNA stability, splicing and translation. Mapping: antibody-based MeRIP-seq/m6A-seq (peaks, ~100–200 nt resolution) and miCLIP (single-nucleotide), antibody-free GLORI and eTAM-seq (quantitative stoichiometry), and <strong>direct RNA nanopore sequencing</strong>, where modifications alter the current signal (m6Anet, CHEUI, xPore, Dorado modification models). Pseudouridine (BID-seq), m5C (bisulfite on RNA) and A-to-I editing are the other common targets. Gap: quantitative, isoform-resolved maps and validation of nanopore-based calls.</p>
<h2>Translatomics</h2>
<p><strong>Ribo-seq</strong> sequences ribosome-protected fragments (~28–30 nt) and shows where ribosomes are, with 3-nt periodicity revealing the reading frame. Uses: translation efficiency (Ribo-seq / RNA-seq; anota2seq, Xtail, riborex, deltaTE), discovery of upstream and small ORFs and micropeptides (RiboCode, ORFquant, PRICE, RiboTISH, Ribo-TISH), and ribosome stalling. QC with RiboseQC and riboWaltz. GENCODE now catalogues Ribo-seq ORFs (Mudge et al. 2022).</p>
<h2>Immunomics and immune repertoires</h2>
<p>T- and B-cell receptors are generated by V(D)J recombination, producing ~10<sup>15</sup> possible receptors. <strong>AIRR-seq</strong> sequences these from bulk or single cells (10x VDJ). Tools: MiXCR, IgBLAST, TRUST4 (from RNA-seq), Immcantation (change-O, SHazaM for somatic hypermutation, clonal lineages), scirpy (single-cell, scverse). Questions: clonal expansion, convergent responses to vaccines and infection, antigen specificity prediction (TCRdist, GLIPH2, and deep models with modest generalisation to unseen epitopes). <strong>Immunopeptidomics</strong> measures HLA-presented peptides by MS to train neoantigen predictors (NetMHCpan, MHCflurry).</p>
<h2>Glycomics and glycoproteomics</h2>
<p>Glycans are branched, non-templated structures attached to proteins and lipids. They control protein folding, cell recognition, immune function (IgG Fc glycosylation tunes antibody effector activity) and are altered in cancer. Analysis: released-glycan MS or HPLC, intact glycopeptide MS (pGlyco, MSFragger-Glyco, Byonic), databases GlyTouCan and GlyGen. Gap: structural isomers are hard to distinguish, and glycan biosynthesis is only indirectly coded in the genome.</p>
<h2>Cell-free DNA, liquid biopsy and fragmentomics</h2>
<p>Dying cells release DNA fragments into blood, with a ~167 bp peak reflecting nucleosome protection. Tumour-derived cfDNA (ctDNA) can be detected through mutations (deep targeted sequencing with UMIs), copy number (low-pass WGS; ichorCNA estimates tumour fraction), methylation patterns that also reveal tissue of origin (multi-cancer early detection tests), and fragment size, end motifs and coverage at TSSs (DELFI and related fragmentomics methods). Non-invasive prenatal testing uses the same principles. Gap: sensitivity for early-stage cancers, where ctDNA fractions can be below 0.1%.</p>
<h2>Interactomics and functional genomics screens</h2>
<p>Pooled <strong>CRISPR screens</strong> (knockout, CRISPRi, CRISPRa, base and prime editing) measure fitness or reporter effects for every gene or variant; analyse with MAGeCK, BAGEL2, CRISPResso2 (editing outcomes) and the DepMap resources (Chronos-corrected gene effects across ~1,000 cancer cell lines). <strong>Perturb-seq</strong> combines CRISPR perturbations with single-cell RNA readouts; genome-scale Perturb-seq (Replogle et al. 2022) maps transcriptional consequences of nearly every expressed gene. <strong>MPRAs</strong> (massively parallel reporter assays) test thousands of regulatory variants for activity at once.</p>
<h2>Phenomics, imaging and clinical omics</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Cell Painting</strong>: multiplexed fluorescent stains and CellProfiler or deep features produce morphological profiles for drug and gene perturbations (JUMP-Cell Painting consortium).</li>
<li><strong>Pathomics</strong>: deep learning on whole-slide H&amp;E images predicts mutations, expression and survival; foundation models (UNI, CONCH, Virchow, Prov-GigaPath) provide general-purpose embeddings.</li>
<li><strong>Radiogenomics</strong>: linking imaging features to molecular subtypes.</li>
<li><strong>EHR phenomics</strong>: phenotype definitions (phecodes, HPO, OMOP common data model) enable PheWAS and biobank-scale genetics.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Exposomics</h2>
<p>The exposome is the totality of environmental exposures over a lifetime: chemicals, diet, pollutants, infections, social factors. High-resolution MS of blood and urine detects thousands of exogenous chemicals; ExWAS (exposome-wide association studies) parallel GWAS. Gaps: chemical identification (even darker than the endogenous metabolome), time-varying exposure, and correlated exposures that confound each other.</p>
