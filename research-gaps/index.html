ResearchChapter 19

Research questions and open gaps

A map of what is unsolved, organised so you can find a thesis-sized problem. Each gap names why it is hard and what a contribution could look like. Strong PhD projects usually sit where a biological question, a data resource and a methodological weakness meet.

Cross-cutting gaps

1 · Causality, not correlation

Most multi-omics results are associations. Combining genetic anchors (QTLs, MR), perturbations (CRISPR, drugs) and time series to build causal multi-layer models is a central open problem.

Contribution: a causal integration method validated against Perturb-seq or CRISPR screens.

2 · Ground truth for evaluation

Many fields (spatial domains, GRNs, multi-omics integration, cell communication) lack ground truth, so methods cannot be compared fairly.

Contribution: a benchmark dataset or calibrated simulator, plus a neutral comparison.

3 · Context specificity

Variant and regulatory effects depend on cell type, state, stimulus and development. Current reference maps cover few contexts.

Contribution: dynamic or single-cell QTL mapping; models that transfer effects across contexts.

4 · Diversity and equity

Reference panels, GWAS, QTL resources and training data for predictors over-represent European ancestry, which limits accuracy elsewhere.

Contribution: multi-ancestry fine-mapping, PRS or QTL methods; analysis of under-represented cohorts.

5 · Foundation models, honestly evaluated

Large pre-trained models for DNA, RNA, proteins and cells are proliferating, but evaluations frequently show they do not beat simple baselines on key tasks.

Contribution: rigorous evaluation, failure analysis, or task-specific models that demonstrably add value.

6 · Uncertainty and calibration

Most pipelines output point estimates. Propagating uncertainty from raw signal to biological conclusion (quantification, imputation, latent factors) is rare.

Contribution: Bayesian or conformal methods that give calibrated intervals.

7 · Interpretability

Deep models predict well but do not explain. Linking latent dimensions to pathways and mechanisms remains hard.

Contribution: biologically constrained architectures (pathway-informed, sparse) with validation.

8 · Scale, privacy and federation

Biobanks with millions of participants and atlases with hundreds of millions of cells strain current algorithms; human data often cannot leave its institution.

Contribution: scalable or federated algorithms, sketching, out-of-core methods.

How to choose a PhD problem in this field

  1. Start from a biological question that someone cares about (a disease, a developmental process, a drug response), then ask which layer limits the answer.
  2. Secure data early: public cohorts (TCGA, GTEx, UK Biobank, CELLxGENE), a collaborator's matched multi-omics, or your own. Many methods projects stall for lack of the right dataset.
  3. Find the methodological weakness using the benchmark hub: where do existing tools disagree, fail on certain data, or lack evaluation?
  4. Plan a validation: an orthogonal experiment, an independent cohort, a held-out perturbation, or a simulation calibrated to real data.
  5. Scope three papers: commonly (a) a benchmark or resource, (b) a method, (c) a biological application of the method.
  6. Release software as a documented package (Bioconductor, PyPI/scverse ecosystem) with tests; it multiplies the impact of the thesis.
Ten concrete starting questions
  1. Do single-cell foundation model embeddings improve cell-type-specific eQTL mapping compared with pseudobulk on annotated clusters?
  2. Can long-read RNA-seq resolve which GWAS-colocalised sQTLs change protein-coding potential?
  3. Which share of GWAS loci without eQTL colocalisation are explained by caQTLs in disease-relevant cell types?
  4. How robust are multi-omics cancer subtypes (MOFA+, SNF, iCluster) across independent cohorts and platforms?
  5. Can cis-pQTL Mendelian randomisation and metabolite QTLs together identify enzyme targets with on-target metabolic biomarkers?
  6. Does integrating phosphoproteomics with transcriptomics (e.g. COSMOS) predict drug response better than either layer alone in cell-line panels?
  7. How much do spatial deconvolution results change with the choice of single-cell reference, and can this uncertainty be modelled?
  8. What fraction of untargeted metabolomic features in human plasma are microbial in origin, and can metagenomes predict them?
  9. Can calibrated variant effect predictors from protein language models reduce VUS rates in under-represented ancestries?
  10. How should a multi-omics study allocate a fixed budget between more samples and more layers?
