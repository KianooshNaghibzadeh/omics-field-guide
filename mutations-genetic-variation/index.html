Foundations

Mutations and genetic variation

A mutation matters because of where in the information flow it acts. The same single-base change can do nothing, break a protein, shift a splice site, disable an enhancer or change a drug's metabolism. This chapter classifies variants and traces their consequences through every layer.

Terms that are often confused

Mutation
A change in DNA sequence relative to a parent cell or organism; an event.
Variant
A position where an individual differs from a reference sequence. Preferred neutral term in clinical genetics.
Polymorphism
A variant common in a population (conventionally minor allele frequency ≥ 1%).
Allele
One version of a sequence at a locus. Humans are diploid: two alleles per autosomal locus.
Genotype / haplotype
The pair of alleles at a locus / the set of alleles on one chromosome copy. Phasing assigns alleles to haplotypes.
Germline / somatic
Inherited and present in every cell / acquired after conception and present only in a cell lineage (tumours, clonal haematopoiesis, mosaicism).
Zygosity
Heterozygous (one copy), homozygous (two), hemizygous (one copy present, e.g. X in males), compound heterozygous (two different variants on different copies).

Variants are written in HGVS nomenclature: NM_000546.6:c.743G>A (cDNA position on a specific transcript) and p.(Arg248Gln) (protein). Always specify the transcript. MANE Select transcripts (agreed by NCBI and EBI) are now the default for clinical reporting.

Non-coding consequences

  • Promoter: TERT promoter mutations (C228T, C250T) create ETS-factor binding sites and reactivate telomerase in melanoma, glioblastoma and bladder cancer.
  • Enhancer: a variant in the ZRS enhancer, ~1 Mb from SHH, causes preaxial polydactyly. The FTO obesity locus acts through enhancers that regulate IRX3/IRX5, not FTO itself.
  • UTRs: variants that create upstream start codons, or that destroy microRNA sites or polyadenylation signals.
  • Structural and 3D: deleting a TAD boundary lets enhancers reach the wrong gene (enhancer hijacking).
  • Non-coding RNA genes: variants in RNU4ATAC (minor spliceosome snRNA) cause developmental disorders; a recurrent RNU4-2 variant is a recently described common cause of neurodevelopmental disorder.

Worked example: lactase persistence

The variant rs4988235 (−13910 C>T) sits in an intron of MCM6, ~14 kb upstream of LCT. The T allele creates an OCT1 binding site in an enhancer (epigenome), keeps LCT transcription active into adulthood (transcriptome), maintains lactase enzyme in the intestinal brush border (proteome), allows lactose digestion into glucose and galactose (metabolome), and produces lactose tolerance (phenome). It was strongly selected in populations with a history of dairying. One variant, six layers.

Somatic mutations and cancer

A tumour is a clonal expansion carrying driver mutations, which confer a growth advantage, and many more passenger mutations, which are neutral hitchhikers. Analyses specific to somatic data include:

  • Tumour–normal paired calling to remove germline variants (Mutect2, Strelka2), with panels of normals to remove recurrent artefacts.
  • Variant allele fraction (VAF) adjusted for purity and copy number to infer clonality and reconstruct phylogenies (PyClone-VI, SciClone, PhyloWGS).
  • Driver discovery through recurrence beyond the background mutation rate (MutSigCV, dNdScv, OncodriveFML), which must correct for replication timing, expression and local sequence context.
  • Mutational signatures: the 96-channel SBS spectrum (6 substitution types × 16 trinucleotide contexts) is decomposed into signatures with known causes. Examples: SBS4 (tobacco smoking, C>A), SBS7 (UV light, C>T at dipyrimidines), SBS2/13 (APOBEC), SBS3 (homologous-recombination deficiency, relevant for PARP inhibitors), SBS1 (clock-like deamination of 5-methylcytosine). Tools: SigProfiler, deconstructSigs, MutationalPatterns, SigLASSO.
  • Tumour mutational burden (TMB) and microsatellite instability (MSI) as biomarkers for immunotherapy; HLA typing and neoantigen prediction (NetMHCpan, pVACtools).

Interpreting a germline variant

The ACMG/AMP 2015 guidelines combine evidence types into five classes: pathogenic, likely pathogenic, variant of uncertain significance (VUS), likely benign, benign. Evidence types include population frequency (gnomAD; a variant too common for the disease prevalence is benign), computational predictions, functional data, segregation in families, de novo status, and the gene–disease relationship (ClinGen). ClinGen's 2022 calibration of computational predictors (Pejaver et al.) specifies score thresholds for tools such as REVEL and BayesDel so they can count as supporting, moderate or strong evidence.

Missense predictors

SIFT and PolyPhen-2 (classic), CADD (genome-wide integrative score), REVEL and BayesDel (ensembles), PrimateAI-3D, EVE and ESM-1v (evolutionary and protein language models, trained without clinical labels), AlphaMissense (AlphaFold-derived, 2023).

Constraint metrics

pLI and LOEUF (gnomAD) measure how strongly a gene tolerates loss-of-function variants. Missense constraint (MPC) and regional constraint highlight intolerant domains. GERP, phyloP and phastCons measure evolutionary conservation per base.

Experimental maps

Deep mutational scanning (DMS) and saturation genome editing (SGE; e.g. BRCA1, Findlay et al. 2018) measure the effect of nearly every possible SNV in a region. MaveDB collects these maps, which are increasingly used as functional evidence.

Pitfall

Predictors trained on ClinVar and evaluated on ClinVar look better than they are, because the same variants or genes leak between training and test sets and because clinical classifications already used older predictors (Grimm et al. 2015, Human Mutation). Prefer evaluations on deep mutational scanning data or on newly classified variants.

Pharmacogenomics

Germline variants in drug-metabolising enzymes and transporters change drug exposure. Examples with clinical guidelines (CPIC, DPWG): CYP2D6 (codeine, tamoxifen; star-allele haplotypes including gene deletions, duplications and hybrids), CYP2C19 (clopidogrel), TPMT/NUDT15 (thiopurines), DPYD (fluoropyrimidines), HLA-B*57:01 (abacavir hypersensitivity), SLCO1B1 (statin myopathy). Calling star alleles requires specialised tools (Aldy, Stargazer, PharmCAT, Cyrius) because of paralogs and structural variants.

Population genetics you will need

  • Allele frequency and Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium: strong deviations in controls often indicate genotyping error.
  • Linkage disequilibrium (LD): nearby variants are inherited together, so a GWAS signal marks a region, not a causal variant. Fine-mapping (SuSiE, FINEMAP) assigns posterior probabilities to variants within the region.
  • Population structure and ancestry: control with principal components or mixed models, otherwise allele-frequency differences between groups create false associations. About 85% or more of GWAS participants have been of European ancestry, which reduces the portability of polygenic scores to other groups.
  • Effect size versus frequency: rare variants can have large effects (Mendelian disease); common variants usually have small effects (complex traits). Both contribute to heritability.
Research gap
  • Most VUSs are missense or non-coding. Accurate, calibrated prediction for non-coding variants is largely unsolved.
  • Cell-type- and context-specific variant effects, including variants that act only under stimulation or at particular developmental stages.
  • Equitable references: allele frequencies, LD panels and predictor training data remain skewed toward European ancestry.
  • Somatic mosaicism in normal tissues: detecting variants at VAF below 1% without error-corrected sequencing.
