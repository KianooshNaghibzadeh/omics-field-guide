A field guide for bioinformaticians · from first principles to open problems

Every layer of the cell, read as data.

This guide walks from the chemistry of DNA to the algorithms that turn sequencers, mass spectrometers and microscopes into biological knowledge. For each omics layer it explains what is measured, why it matters, how the data are processed, which tools do each step, what independent benchmarks found, and which questions are still open.

The layers, top to bottom

Information flows down this stack, from the relatively stable genome to the fast-changing metabolome. The bar shows roughly how dynamic each layer is (how fast it changes within a cell or person). Click a layer to open its chapter.

Genome
The DNA sequence: ~3.1 billion base pairs per haploid human genome, ~20,000 protein-coding genes. Mostly fixed for life, except somatic mutation.
DNA · years–lifetime
Epigenome
Chemical marks and packaging that decide which DNA is readable: CpG methylation, histone modifications, accessibility, 3D folding.
chromatin · days–years
Transcriptome
All RNA molecules: mRNAs and their isoforms, lncRNAs, miRNAs. A snapshot of which genes are being used right now.
RNA · minutes–hours
Proteome
Proteins, proteoforms and their modifications, abundances, locations and interactions: the cell's working machinery.
protein · hours–days
Metabolome
Small molecules (<1,500 Da): sugars, amino acids, lipids, drugs. The closest molecular readout of phenotype.
metabolite · seconds–minutes
Microbiome
Genomes and functions of the microbes living with us; a second genome that shapes our metabolome and immunity.
community · days–months
Phenome
Traits, disease and clinical outcomes. Multi-omics is ultimately about explaining how variation at the top produces variation here.
organism · integrated

How to read the tool tables

Every chapter lists the main tools for that layer. Each tool carries two kinds of "level", because people mean both when they ask about a tool's level.

Analysis stage

Primary Raw instrument signal to usable reads or spectra: base calling, demultiplexing, signal processing.

Secondary Reads or spectra to features: alignment, quantification, variant calling, peak calling, identification.

Tertiary Features to biology: statistics, differential analysis, annotation, networks, integration, interpretation.

Skill level

●●● Entry: GUI, web server or one-command pipeline; sensible defaults.

●●● Intermediate: scripting in R or Python, choosing parameters, understanding the model.

●●● Advanced: method-level understanding, HPC or GPU, custom models, many parameters that change the answer.

About this guide

Tool versions and benchmark rankings change quickly. Benchmarks are cited by first author, year and journal; each citation links to a PubMed search so you can read the original and check for newer work. When a statement is a judgement rather than a published result, the text says so.
